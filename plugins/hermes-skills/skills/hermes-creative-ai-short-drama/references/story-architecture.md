# Story Architecture

Use this reference when creating or extending a serialized short-drama project. Keep only facts that affect story choices or production continuity. Exact mechanisms, dialogue, names, and decorative lore stay open until they change causality, character choice, or production.

## Greenlight contract

Before outlining, state:

- **premise:** protagonist + pressure + distinctive mechanism + irreversible goal;
- **audience promise:** the repeatable emotion/payoff and episode grammar;
- **escalation runway:** how the burden changes type rather than merely growing louder;
- **production repeatability:** recurring characters, locations, assets, and visual grammar that can be regenerated consistently;
- **central cost:** what cannot be preserved, solved, or obtained simultaneously;
- **pilot differentiation:** at least three materially different ways the premise can generate drama without repeating the same case with new props.

Choose `GO_PILOT`, `REVISE`, or `STOP`. Stop or revise when escalation runway, production repeatability, or episode-to-episode differentiation is missing.

## Architecture before detail

Develop in this order:

`series question -> character functions -> world limits -> causal engine -> progression tracks -> episode function -> concrete event -> mechanism -> dialogue`

Do not reverse the order because a clever mechanism or line sounds exciting.

Before locking a detailed mechanism, ask:

1. Does this detail change the choice?
2. Does it change who is responsible?
3. Does it create or repay a setup?
4. Does production need it fixed now?
5. Does plausibility require validation now?

If all answers are no, keep it `TBD` or `VALIDATE_LATER`. The internal causal model may be more detailed than what appears on screen.

## Series Bible

The Series Bible is the canonical persistent story record. It contains:

- approved premise, audience promise, and repeatable episode grammar;
- character IDs, wants, false beliefs or systemic constraints, leverage, authority boundaries, relationships, and production anchors;
- world/system rules, including limits and costs;
- recurring location and entity IDs;
- progression tracks appropriate to the series;
- Payoff Debt Ledger: promises/injustices opened, due window, and payment state;
- recurring pressure carriers or moral mirrors when useful;
- current canonical state and unresolved `TBD` / `NEEDS_REVIEW` items.

Approved persistent facts are immutable unless an explicit revision is recorded. Do not silently reset relationships, knowledge, injuries, possessions, rules, or paid debts.

## Use multiple progression tracks when the story needs them

Do not force all change into a single Reveal Ladder. Separate tracks by function.

### 1. Reveal / Knowledge Ladder

Tracks what the audience and characters understand.

A strong major reveal often develops as:

`signal -> repetition -> old explanation fails -> new interpretation becomes actionable`

Do not spend half a season hiding the premise when the premise itself is the selling point. Reveal the mechanism early enough that later suspense can become: **what do they do now that they understand it?**

### 2. Pressure / Public-Action Ladder

Tracks what kind of burden a decision creates. Escalation should change the question, not just add bodies or explosions.

A generic pattern can move through:

`physical action -> evidence/uncertainty -> consequences for others -> informed opposition -> authority/legitimacy -> private cost`

Adapt or replace the steps for the series. The rule is that each step changes what makes the decision hard.

### 3. Relationship / Capability Ladder

Tracks changes that are neither “new information” nor “bigger danger”:

- trust;
- dependency;
- authority;
- access to the world;
- ability to act;
- shared experience;
- who must rely on whose perception.

Prefer gradual observable changes over a late explanatory announcement. A capability loss can be shown as shrinking reach or stale information while cognition/personality remain intact.

Add only the tracks the story needs. Name project-specific ladders in the Series Bible rather than hard-coding them into the Skill.

## Episode Function Map

Before planning Episode N, summarize Episodes 1..N-1 with at least:

| Episode | Dominant turn | Conflict grammar | Pressure type | Reveal/progression | Payoff/callback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then state the proposed episode's **new function** in one sentence.

Reject or revise the proposal when its grammar is functionally the same as an earlier episode, even if the surface changes. Examples of false novelty:

- another door/bridge/train with the same “systems disagree -> verify minimum facts -> let people through” function;
- another casualty used to repeat an already-proven moral point;
- the same two-timeline reveal merely moved to a different event;
- another “AI fails -> human finishes alone” beat after the relationship has already advanced beyond that question.

Repeat visual motifs or locations only when their **meaning changes**.

## One dominant turn, multiple converging lines

Each episode has exactly one dominant turn.

A/B lines are allowed when they converge on the same turn, cost, or thematic action. Do not stack two independent episodes into one runtime.

Useful convergence forms:

- public decision + private mirror;
- present action + past timeline that changes the meaning of the present action;
- field execution + authorization process for the same irreversible choice.

If removing one line leaves the other unchanged, they may be separate episodes rather than one integrated episode.

## Callbacks: causal, not decorative

Prefer returning to an earlier person, place, object, rule, or infrastructure when later consequences can change its meaning.

A strong callback does at least one:

- turns a previous solution into a later problem;
- makes a previous beneficiary become evidence, opposition, or cost;
- revisits the same place under a harder question;
- applies an earlier public principle to the protagonist's private stake;
- changes a motif from one form of connection/loss/authority into another.

Do not add callbacks solely for recognition.

## Opposition without a villain

An antagonist ladder does not require an evil person.

Pressure may come from:

- two valid institutions;
- incompatible public duties;
- residents defending a life that earlier decisions helped create;
- a competent system applying a rule correctly;
- a former ally whose interests have rationally changed;
- a moral mirror who accepts the same facts but rejects the protagonist's conclusion.

Do not invent a conspiracy or final boss if the core premise is stronger as a conflict among legitimate realities/interests.

A recurring pressure carrier is often stronger than disposable opposition: the same reasonable character can first request help, later benefit from the protagonists' decision, then oppose the next-stage policy because its cost has reached them.

## Third paths still cost

A third path may reject a false A/B choice, but it may not erase the central cost.

When a new option appears, record:

- which assumption it rejects;
- why it is actually available;
- what becomes worse, smaller, slower, irreversible, or lost;
- who owns the decision to accept that cost.

A tactical success may still be a strategic failure. This is often stronger than “the smart character finds the perfect answer.”

## Theme hierarchy

Keep these layers distinct:

1. **highest theme / moral question** — what the whole series is interrogating;
2. **series mechanism** — the repeatable structural problem that makes the theme playable;
3. **governance/strategy** — how characters try to respond over time;
4. **episode action** — the concrete choice visible today;
5. **motif** — recurring image, object, phrase, weather, gesture, etc.

Do not mistake a mechanism or slogan for the highest theme. Do not require characters to say the theme aloud.

## Explanation budget

Lock backstage causality strongly; expose it sparingly.

Use this priority:

`visible consequence -> actionable fact -> decision -> only then necessary explanation`

Do not put the full research path, permissions chain, data-source list, engineering derivation, or philosophical interpretation on screen when one causal sentence or visual consequence is enough.

“Why the solution is valid” may be locked in canon without showing “how the character searched every source to find it.”

## Finale / private-cost gate

Before a finale sacrifice or irreversible private loss, verify:

1. the governing principle existed before the finale;
2. the audience saw it applied to someone or something else first;
3. the private cost is the same principle returning at closer range, not a new rule invented to hurt the protagonist;
4. the affected character retains agency and a survival preference when appropriate;
5. voluntary acceptance by one character does not erase another character's independent moral choice;
6. the ending does not secretly undo the cost with an unearned copy, upload, exception, or “actually survived” loophole unless that is the established premise.

The finale can resolve the action while leaving moral discomfort intact.

## Pilot first

Default to a 3–5 episode pilot. Each episode must test a distinct part of the promise and contain one dominant turn. Do not expand the full season until the pilot proves:

1. the premise is understandable quickly;
2. the payoff mechanism works more than once;
3. the same grammar is not merely being reskinned;
4. escalation changes choices, authority, relationships, or cost;
5. the production design is repeatable;
6. the ending creates a genuine next-episode question.

## Episode shape

Use this flexible order for a roughly one- to three-minute episode:

`Cold Open -> Pressure -> Turn -> Payoff/Progress -> Cliffhanger/End Image -> State Delta`

- **Cold Open** makes the immediate imbalance, anomaly, or ordinary-life fracture visible.
- **Pressure** forces a choice; context exists only to make that choice legible.
- **Turn** is the episode's single dominant change in power, information, relationship, goal, or cost.
- **Payoff/Progress** either repays a debt or moves a promised thread measurably forward.
- **Cliffhanger/End Image** creates a consequential prediction or a changed meaning, not an arbitrary sentence cut.
- **State Delta** records only persistent changes that actually occurred.

## Ladders and ledgers

- Reveal/Knowledge Ladder prevents dumping or artificially withholding the core truth.
- Pressure/Public-Action Ladder changes the kind of decision burden or opposition.
- Relationship/Capability Ladder tracks trust, dependency, access, or authority when relevant.
- Payoff Debt Ledger records `debt_id`, opened episode, promise/injustice, due window, current state, and paid episode. A pilot must repay at least one small early debt.
- Entity Registry gives stable IDs to recurring characters, locations, props, clues, and production-relevant states.

## State inheritance

To start Episode N+1, load:

1. the latest Series Bible canonical state;
2. explicit canon revision patches that supersede older scope;
3. Episode N's approved State Delta;
4. Episode N's Continuation Capsule;
5. unresolved payoff debts, motifs/callbacks, and progression constraints.

Apply the approved State Delta to the canonical state, then use the Continuation Capsule as the compact writing handoff. If they conflict, stop and mark the episode `NEEDS_REVIEW`; do not choose a convenient reset.

## Continuation Capsule

Keep the capsule short enough to write the next episode without rereading the whole transcript. Include:

- current dramatic question and immediate pressure;
- character knowledge, relationship, capability/access, and goal changes;
- prop/clue ownership and reveal state;
- location, costume, injury, or time facts that constrain the opening;
- unresolved payoff debts and next allowed progression;
- callbacks/motifs that are due but not yet paid;
- the last visible action/image that the next episode must reconnect to;
- any approved function whose mechanism/dialogue remains `TBD`.
