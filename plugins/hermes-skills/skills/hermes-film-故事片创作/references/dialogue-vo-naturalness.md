# Dialogue and Voice-over Naturalness

Use this reference whenever writing or revising dialogue/VO, or when feedback includes “怪”“不是人话”“太像宣传片”“旁白太多”“解释太多”.

## 1. Dialogue priority

A character normally speaks to:
1. handle the immediate situation;
2. affect another person;
3. maintain or change a relationship;
4. transfer necessary information;
5. reveal themselves.

Directly stating the theme is normally last.

## 2. 人话五检

For every important line, check:

### 口腔测试
Can a real person say it naturally in one breath?

### 场景测试
Would this person say this here and now, rather than in an interview or essay?

### 身份测试
Could a different character say the same line unchanged? If yes, the voice may be generic.

### 抽象词测试
If one sentence stacks multiple abstractions such as “意义/精神/责任/价值/传承/选择”, rewrite toward concrete facts, needs, or judgments.

### 作者测试
Is the character speaking, or is the writer borrowing the character to summarize the movie?

## 3. Formal terms are not automatically empty language

Do not ban professional, institutional, or official terminology simply because it sounds formal.

Ask instead:
- Is the term necessary and accurate?
- Is this the right speaker and context?
- Does the term carry specific professional meaning?
- Is it replacing a fact that should be shown or explained more concretely?

The failure mode is unsupported abstraction, not formality itself.

## 4. Connection-word alert

Words such as “其实”“反而”“说到底”“后来我才发现”“真正让我……的是” are not forbidden. They become a warning when repeated to manufacture reflection or when the line reads like written commentary rather than speech.

Prefer:
- concrete fact + direct judgment;
- an action that makes the judgment unnecessary;
- a shorter statement that leaves the scene doing part of the work.

## 5. Voice-over only carries a limited set of jobs

**旁白只承担** these core functions:

1. **Frame** — why the protagonist is here / what they are trying to do.
2. **Psychological clarification** — a change would otherwise be materially misunderstood.
3. **Time compression** — days, phases, repeated observations.
4. **Final definition** — when the project mode requires an explicit concluding value judgment.

VO should not become a running explanation track for every scene.

## 6. Picture / VO redundancy check

For each VO sentence ask:

> If this sentence disappears, does the picture already communicate the same information and judgment?

If yes, remove or shorten it.

If the picture gives the fact but the protagonist’s reason would otherwise be ambiguous, keep only the shortest needed psychological clarification.

## 7. “Memory line” versus fake golden line

A memorable line earns its place when it:
- comes from the character’s situation;
- could plausibly be said by that character;
- changes or crystallizes a relationship/decision;
- gains meaning from surrounding action.

A fake golden line usually:
- summarizes the theme for the audience;
- could be pasted into many unrelated films;
- is polished but not situational;
- repeats what the scene already proved.

Do not score a screenplay by the number of quotable lines.

## 8. Register defaults

These are defaults, not stereotypes:
- frontline/professional workers: concise, task-linked, context-dependent;
- younger staff: somewhat fuller language, still situational;
- family members: life arrangements, relationships, needs, frustration/support;
- leaders/managers: task, standard, time, result, decision;
- creator/protagonist VO: may be more complete, but should still sound like a person thinking, not a director’s essay.

## 9. Final read-aloud pass

Read every dialogue/VO line aloud and ask:
- Is this how this person would phrase it?
- Is the sentence more complete than the situation requires?
- Is the writer explaining the theme?
- Is the picture already saying it?
- Can the line become more direct without losing meaning?
