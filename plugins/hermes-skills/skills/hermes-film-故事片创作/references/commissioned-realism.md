# Commissioned Realism and Industry Storytelling

Use this reference for real-person adaptation, enterprise/industry commissioned microfilms, public-service or institutional narrative films, and documentary-fiction hybrids.

## 1. Project-mode rule

Real/commissioned work is not ordinary fiction with company names added.

Priority is:
- factual credibility;
- professional role credibility;
- human behavior;
- causal drama;
- shootability and actor feasibility;
- only then stylistic flourish.

## 2. Where conflict may come from

Positive professional characters do not need to become hostile or incompetent for the film to have drama.

Useful conflict sources include:
- protagonist expectation vs real-world process;
- personal objective vs time/resource constraints;
- two legitimate objectives competing;
- information arriving late or incompletely;
- authority/permission boundaries;
- work priorities vs filming/communication priorities;
- a professional judgment that cannot responsibly be rushed.

Do not automatically manufacture a villain, cold treatment, quarrel, violation, accident, or emergency just to increase stakes.

## 3. Professional fact states

Apply the shared states from revision control:
- LOCKED
- CONFIRMED
- TENTATIVE
- CONFLICT
- INFERRED
- **DO_NOT_INVENT**

Technical operations, safety procedures, historical facts, confidential facts, medical/legal/professional actions, and authority boundaries frequently belong in DO_NOT_INVENT until verified.

When exact procedure is not verified, write only the level the evidence supports: visible state, communication, movement, confirmation, consequence, record/review.

## 4. Permission and knowledge boundaries

For each professional scene, ask:
- Who has permission to perform this action?
- Who is only observing or filming?
- Can this person leave the post at this time?
- Could this character know this information yet?
- Is equipment/personnel allowed in this location?
- Would this action be repeated for filming, or is re-enactment unsafe/unrealistic?

Do not use narration to repair an impossible action.

## 5. Continuity ledger

For accuracy-sensitive stories, maintain at least:

| Scene | Relative time | Shift/status | Location | Present characters | Key fact/object | Result carried forward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Check transport time, shift transitions, equipment state, costume/physical continuity, information flow, and unresolved operational consequences.

## 6. Documentary-fiction hybrid

Explicitly distinguish:
- dramatized narrative;
- real documentary footage/interview;
- historical/archive material.

The transition between modes needs a narrative reason. A real-documentary ending should complete or reframe the dramatic question, not act as a leftover-material montage.

Real interviews should use authentic approved wording. Do not invent quotable statements and present them as documentary truth.

## 7. Non-professional actors

Many commissioned films use employees or other non-professional performers.

Prefer:
- familiar real actions;
- short situational lines;
- simple blocking;
- observable decisions;
- natural pauses and interaction.

Treat these as higher-risk:
- long monologues;
- precise emotional breakdowns;
- dense subtext exchanges;
- overlapping dialogue requiring exact timing;
- dangerous-action reenactment.

If a real behavior can carry the scene, do not require the performer to “act the theme”.

## 8. Family/personal-life material

Personal cost is evidence of a life, not automatically a theme.

Avoid stacking suffering simply to increase emotional weight. If family is relevant, prefer a complete relationship picture: practical arrangements, frustration, understanding/support, ordinary needs, and boundaries.

Family members should not be used as corporate spokespeople unless that is genuinely their role and approved wording.

## 9. History/archive material

Historical material must change the audience’s understanding of the present story. If it does not affect a current action, relationship, decision, or value question, shorten it or move it out of the dramatic spine.

Do not claim two professional incidents are “the same” without evidence supporting that equivalence.

## 10. Ending rule

For open/authorial fiction, the audience may complete the final judgment.

For a commissioned film that explicitly requires a clear value conclusion, the film may define the point after evidence is established.

Preferred order:
**action evidence → character choice → consequence/aftermath → shortest necessary definition → approved end framing**.

Do not announce the conclusion first and then use scenes as decoration.
